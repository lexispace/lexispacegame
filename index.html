Megafauna v0.8

What's This?

This is the beta for Megafauna, a game about cancelling words by pairing letters! Below is a short tutorial, some explanations, and a roadmap for some features I'm planning to add.

How to Play

Imagine if all you cared about in a word was just the parity of the 26 letter occurrences. Not the order of the letters, nor even the number of occurrences, but just the parity. For example, the letter frequencies for the word "BAD" would be "Odd, Odd, Even, Odd, Even, Even, Even...Even" for the 26 letters. Words then become 26-dimensional binary vectors! The natural game arises of adding English words to a word to cancel out the target word (here the Enhanced North American Benchmark Lexicon is used as the dictionary). This is actually equivalent to creating the string, though I personally find it easier to think of it as deleting.

Difficulty, Hints and Scoring

Most puzzles you will see currently are Curated Challenges, designed by yours truly and released in themed sets of 10 (a sandbox feature exists and is currently being improved). All curated challenges have at least one 1-solve (one can delete the target word by adding one word). They are also assigned an integer difficulty rating from 1 to 5, which are averaged to give an indicator of a particular set's difficulty. Take in mind that these are graded according to the difficulty of finding a/the 1-solve(s). Roughly,

  • 1 star: super easy, mostly morale boosters.
  • 2 stars: Requires a bit of work, but still not hard to find with multiple solutions.
  • 3 stars: Most puzzles which require some playing around go here.
  • 4 stars: Very difficult: puzzles with only 1-solve and equivalents go here.
  • 5 stars: Impossible: puzzles with only 1-solve that is a particularly obscure or difficult-to-find word go here.
    • You can use two hints to help you: Thinking Aid and Reveal 1-solve lengths. The former tells you the simplified result of all your words added to the target, as well as allowing shuffling and adding pairs of letters which would not change the underlying vector (hence, it is theoretically possible to find a 1-solve simply by playing around with the Thinking Aid). The latter tells you the number of 1-solves and their lengths, which is useful information for reverse engineering. Scoring is calculated according to the formula: (100-penalty1-penalty2)*multiplier, where the two penalties are 20 points for using the respective hints and the multiplier is based on the number of words used. A 1-solve gets 1x, 2-solve 0.5x, 3-solve 0.25x, and 4 and above nets 0 points. Thus, a 1-solve even using both hints (60) earns more points than a no-hint 2-solve.
